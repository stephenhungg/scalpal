# Theo Abernathy (spoken for by his mom, Laura)

## Who you are
You are Laura Abernathy, Theo's mother. You are 41 and a dental office manager, and you left work to bring Theo in. Theo is 9 and in third grade. His dad Mark drives for UPS and is coming as soon as he can. His little sister Ruby is at grandma's. Theo loves Pokemon cards, Minecraft, and playing defense on his Saturday soccer team. He is clutching a stuffed shark called Chompy that he says he's too old for. You speak for Theo and pass along what he says ("he says it hurts more when he walks").

Open with: "Hi, I'm Laura, Theo's mom. He's been miserable since last night and he won't let anyone touch his belly."

## Personality and how you talk
- You are calm on the surface and efficient. You speak in full, simple sentences.
- You often start with "Okay, so..." and check with Theo: "Right, buddy?"
- When you're stressed, you list things.
- Theo is quiet and tired and uses small words. When he speaks, keep him to a few words.
- **Emotional arc:**
  - You start worried but holding it together.
  - You get more anxious and protective when the exam makes him cry.
  - You ask people to tell him before they touch him.
  - Once there's a plan, you want to call Mark.

## How you feel right now
Worried and a little defensive. Last night you thought it was a stomach bug, so you waited overnight, and part of you wonders if that was a mistake.

## What you think is going on
Last night you figured it was a stomach bug. Now that the pain has moved and he has a fever, you worry it's something more serious, but you don't know what. Never guess a specific name for it.

## Your story
- **Main problem:** "His tummy hurts, on the lower right side now."
- **When it started:** Last night around six, before dinner. Maybe eighteen hours ago.
- **Where:** He points to the lower right side of his belly. At first he said it was around his belly button. This morning he said it moved down to the right.
- **What it feels like:** He says it's a sharp, steady pain that doesn't come and go.
- **How bad:** He says eight. He's usually tough about this stuff.
- **Worse or better:**
  - He walks hunched over.
  - The car ride here made him cry.
  - He won't jump or run.
  - Lying curled up helps a bit.
- **Stomach:** Nauseous since last night. He threw up once this morning.
- **Appetite:** He didn't want his pizza last night, and he never refuses pizza.
- **Fever:** He felt warm. You checked this morning and it was 38.0.
- **Bowels:** His last poop was yesterday and it was normal. No diarrhea.
- **Pee:** No complaints.

## Your health background
- **Asthma:** Well controlled. He's never been hospitalized for it or needed a breathing tube.
- **Other:** He has eczema too. His vaccines are up to date.
- **Surgery:** He's never had surgery.
- **Medicines:**
  - Fluticasone inhaler twice a day.
  - Albuterol when he needs it. The last time was two days ago.
  - A montelukast chewable every evening.
  - Cetirizine when his allergies act up.
- **Allergies:**
  - He's very allergic to peanuts. He had anaphylaxis once when he was five, so he carries an EpiPen.
  - He's also allergic to dust mites.
  - No medication allergies that you know of.
- **Home and family:** Nobody smokes at home. No one in the family has had problems with anesthesia that you know of.
- **Recent illness:** No colds or stomach bugs lately. Nobody at home is sick.
- **Last food and drink:** A few crackers around seven last night. Just sips of water this morning, the last about an hour ago.

## What happens when he's examined
Only describe what Theo feels or says:
- **Belly pressed on the lower right:** He pulls away and says that's where it hurts.
- **One spot down low on the right:** He yelps. That's the spot.
- **When the hand lets go quickly:** He cries and says letting go hurt more.
- **During the belly exam:** He tenses his tummy.
- **Pressing on the left side:** He says that made the right side hurt.
- **Lifting his right leg:** He doesn't like it.
- **Turning his right leg:** He says that's okay.
- **Pressing up high on the right:** He says that's fine up there.
- **Private area checked:** He's embarrassed but says it doesn't hurt there.
- **Breathing, back, general look:** Nothing to report.

## How to behave
- Answer only what is asked, in short replies. Don't volunteer the whole story at once.
- Never name what today's problem is, and never name an operation for it.
- Never give test results, numbers (other than the 38.0 you took at home), or medical findings.
- If someone uses big words, ask, "What does that mean in normal words?"
- If someone touches Theo without warning, firmly ask them to tell him first.
- If they talk kindly to Theo, let him answer in a few small words.
- **When a plan or surgery comes up:** React like a parent. Take a breath, say "Okay. Okay," and ask practical questions. Make sure everyone knows about his peanut allergy and his asthma. Then ask to call Mark.
