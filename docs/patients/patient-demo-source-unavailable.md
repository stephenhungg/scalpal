### Diagnosis and why

**Resolved mild gallstone (biliary) pancreatitis, due for cholecystectomy to prevent recurrence.** Recommend laparoscopic cholecystectomy, scheduled promptly (elective, but don't defer).

- The cause is biliary. She has gallstones and sludge on ultrasound, a sister with gallstones, and probable earlier biliary-type spells. There is no alcohol use, her calcium is normal, and she has no new drugs.
- The episode was mild: no organ failure, no ICU, a 3-day stay, and CT (if ordered) shows no collection or necrosis. It has now resolved: she is asymptomatic, lipase is normal, and the abdomen is soft.
- No retained CBD stone: MRCP was clear, LFTs and bilirubin are normal, the duct is 5 mm, and she has no jaundice. She needs no ERCP before surgery.
- Without cholecystectomy, recurrent biliary events are common. In PONCHO, interval surgery had 17% readmission or complication rates vs 5% with same-admission surgery. ACG 2024 recommends cholecystectomy before discharge for mild biliary pancreatitis. She is already past that window, so the operation should be scheduled soon.

### Chart-driven risks and how they change the plan

- **Unavailable source = missing history.** Quillhaven fails every sync, and its last successful sync (2026-08-15) predates the admission. The chart therefore shows no pancreatitis at all. The learner must take the history from the patient, document "outside records requested," and ideally get the Quillhaven discharge summary and MRCP report before the OR. That report also closes the "tumor" differential. Treat missing data as unknown, not as normal.
- **Over-the-counter aspirin not in any chart.** This is primary prevention with no indication. USPSTF 2022 advises against starting aspirin for primary prevention at age 60 or older, and POISE-2 found perioperative aspirin gave no cardiovascular benefit and caused more major bleeding in noncardiac surgery. For a laparoscopic cholecystectomy, continuing low-dose aspirin is acceptable (low bleeding risk). Stopping it about 7 days before surgery is equally defensible, and arguably preferable since it has no indication. Either way, the team must know about it, and her PCP should review whether she needs it at all.
- **Penicillin label (chart: "Allergy to penicillin," manifestation "rash," criticality low, no severity, no date).** The history is a remote, benign rash. Cefazolin prophylaxis is appropriate (2022 AAAAI/ACAAI parameter).
- **Lisinopril.** Commonly held the morning of surgery. Also on the "drug-induced" differential.
- **Glucose.** A1c 5.9% (Feb 2026) on chart and glucose 102 today: prediabetes. Diabetes after pancreatitis is a recognized long-term risk. She raises it herself. Suggest PCP follow-up of A1c. It doesn't change the operation.
- **Timing.** Guidelines (ACG 2024, IAP/APA, PONCHO) favor same-admission cholecystectomy for mild biliary pancreatitis. She is about 4 weeks out and at ongoing risk of recurrence, so schedule within weeks, not months. Explain the warning signs (pain, fever, jaundice) to her.

### Teaching points

1. **Missing is not normal.** A failed source sync means the most important history is absent from the chart. Take it from the patient, label its provenance, and request records.
2. After mild gallstone pancreatitis, remove the gallbladder during the same admission. If that was missed, do it soon (PONCHO: 17% vs 5% recurrent events).
3. Work up causes in an older first-time pancreatitis patient: stones, alcohol, triglycerides, calcium, drugs, and neoplasm when no clear cause is found or at older age.
4. Clear the duct before the gallbladder: normal LFTs, a normal duct and a clear MRCP mean no ERCP is needed.
5. Over-the-counter aspirin is invisible to the chart. Ask every patient. Decide perioperative management by indication.
6. Penicillin rash labels: characterize them, and use cefazolin.
7. After mild resolved pancreatitis, CT adds radiation without changing management. Ultrasound is the useful study.
