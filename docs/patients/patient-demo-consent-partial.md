## 2. CLINICIAN KEY (never given to the patient agent; for Jarvis and authors)

### Case summary

Sam Ortiz is a 61-year-old retired bus driver and former 30-pack-year smoker (quit 10 years ago) with hypertension and hyperlipidemia. He shared only medications (lisinopril 10 mg daily, atorvastatin 20 mg at bedtime) and one allergy (penicillin, rash, low criticality, recorded 2015) with the app. There are no demographics, problems, labs or vitals on the chart. He reports 5 to 6 attacks over six months, the last two weeks ago. Each was steady, pressing right upper quadrant pain (7/10), sometimes radiating to the right shoulder, triggered by fried or large dinners, resolving on its own within 1 to 2 hours, with nausea and no vomiting. He has had no fever, jaundice, dark urine or pale stools. He had an open right inguinal hernia repair 15 years ago with uneventful anesthesia. Exam between attacks is benign: soft, minimal deep RUQ tenderness, negative Murphy sign, no recurrent hernia. Labs are normal. Ultrasound shows multiple gallstones up to 15 mm, a 3 mm wall, no pericholecystic fluid and a 4 mm CBD.

### Diagnosis and why

**Symptomatic cholelithiasis (biliary colic).** Recommend elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy.

- The pain pattern is classic: postprandial, steady, RUQ with right shoulder referral, lasting 1 to 2 hours, recurrent and stereotyped.
- No inflammation: afebrile, normal WBC and CRP, Murphy negative, normal wall, no fluid. Acute cholecystitis is excluded (TG18).
- No duct stone: normal LFTs, a 4 mm duct and no jaundice put him at low risk, so he can go straight to cholecystectomy.
- Recurrent attacks over six months justify elective removal.

### Chart-driven risks and how they change the plan

- **Partial consent = unknown, not none.** The chart has no name, DOB, sex, problems, labs, vitals, encounters or immunizations. FinchNode returns 403 `consent_scope_exceeded` for any other category. Don't try to read more. Ask him. Practically, this means:
  - **Identity:** confirm name and DOB in person, and use two identifiers at the time out. The app's patient label is "Unnamed patient (limited chart)."
  - **History:** take the problem list directly (cardiac, pulmonary, renal, diabetes, bleeding, anesthesia).
  - **Labs:** baseline BMP (on lisinopril), LFTs (duct-stone screen), CBC.
  - **Vitals:** get a baseline. None on the chart.
  - **Documentation:** record that history is patient-reported and that he declined broader record sharing. Respect that choice.
- **Penicillin label** (chart: "Allergy to penicillin," manifestation "rash," criticality low, recorded 2015-08-20). The history is a remote benign rash at about age 19. Cefazolin prophylaxis is appropriate.
- **Lisinopril.** Commonly held the morning of surgery. He took it today, which is irrelevant for clinic but should be noted for scheduling instructions.
- **Atorvastatin.** Continue perioperatively.
- **Former heavy smoker (about 30 pack-years).** Higher perioperative pulmonary and cardiac risk. Ask about exertional tolerance (good, so more than 4 METs). An ECG is reasonable at his age and risk profile. Encourage incentive spirometry. He's not currently smoking, so no cessation counseling is needed, but he qualifies for lung cancer screening (USPSTF 2021: age 50 to 80, 20 or more pack-years, quit within 15 years). - **Prior open right inguinal hernia repair.** Right lower quadrant adhesions are unlikely to affect umbilical or subcostal ports. Prior anesthesia was uneventful.

### Teaching points

1. **Consent is a clinical constraint.** Partial sharing means unknown, not normal. Ask the patient. Don't work around the consent.
2. **Confirm identity in person** when the chart can't. Use two identifiers at the time out.
3. **Biliary colic vs cholecystitis** comes down to duration, fever, Murphy sign and ultrasound wall or fluid.
4. **Risk-stratify for a duct stone** with LFTs plus duct size before cholecystectomy.
5. **Penicillin labels:** characterize them, and use cefazolin.
6. **In older smokers with epigastric or RUQ pain,** keep cardiac ischemia on the list and get an ECG. Use the visit to flag lung cancer screening eligibility.
