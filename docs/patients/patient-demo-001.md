### Case summary

Morgan Rivera is a 38-year-old teacher with type 2 diabetes (metformin, dose doubled in July 2026) and hypertension (lisinopril). She is seen in surgery clinic, pain-free, after four stereotyped attacks over about three months. Each was steady, severe (8/10), right upper quadrant and epigastric pain, sometimes radiating to the right scapula. Each started within about 20 minutes of a large or fatty evening meal and resolved on its own within 2 to 3 hours. She had nausea and vomited once. She has had no fever, jaundice, dark urine or pale stools. Exam between attacks is benign: soft abdomen, minimal deep RUQ tenderness and a negative Murphy sign. Labs are normal. Ultrasound shows multiple mobile gallstones up to 12 mm, a 2 mm wall, no pericholecystic fluid, a negative sonographic Murphy sign and a 4 mm common bile duct. Urine hCG is negative. Her mother had a cholecystectomy in her forties. Her penicillin label is a remote, mild childhood rash, and she has since tolerated cephalexin.

### Diagnosis and why

**Symptomatic cholelithiasis (biliary colic).** Recommend elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy.

- The pain is the classic biliary pattern. It is steady, not colicky, starts within about an hour of a fatty meal, is RUQ or epigastric with scapular radiation, and resolves within about 1 to 5 hours. StatPearls describes typical attacks as lasting 1 to 5 hours, and pain beyond about 6 hours suggests cholecystitis.
- She has no fever, leukocytosis, raised CRP, wall thickening, pericholecystic fluid or Murphy sign. Acute cholecystitis is excluded: she does not meet Tokyo 2018 (TG18) A+B+C criteria.
- Normal bilirubin and alkaline phosphatase plus a 4 mm duct make choledocholithiasis unlikely (low risk). She needs no MRCP or ERCP before surgery.
- Four attacks in three months, with recurrence despite diet change, justify elective removal. Symptomatic gallstones recur and carry risk of complications such as cholecystitis, duct stones and pancreatitis.

- **Penicillin label (chart: "Penicillin," rash, mild, criticality low, recorded 2021).** The history shows a benign childhood rash and later uneventful cephalexin. The 2022 AAAAI/ACAAI drug allergy practice parameter supports giving cefazolin even with a history of penicillin anaphylaxis, because cefazolin's R1 side chain is dissimilar. She should get standard cefazolin prophylaxis if prophylaxis is used. Note that routine prophylaxis for low-risk elective lap chole is optional. Consider referral for formal delabeling. An unexamined label drives vancomycin or clindamycin use and higher surgical site infection rates.

- **Diabetes control.** Chart A1c trend is 7.1% (Jan) to 6.8% (Apr) to 6.4% (Jul 2026). That is well controlled and not a reason to delay. Diabetes is also why an atypical cardiac presentation was worth one question.

### Teaching points

1. Biliary "colic" is a misnomer. The pain is steady. Duration is the key discriminator: under about 6 hours suggests colic, persistent pain with fever suggests cholecystitis.
2. Ultrasound is the first test for gallstones. CT misses many cholesterol stones.
3. Use LFTs plus duct diameter to stratify risk of a common bile duct stone before cholecystectomy (ASGE risk categories). She is low risk, so she can go straight to surgery.
4. A penicillin "allergy" is usually not a true allergy. Characterize the reaction. Cefazolin is safe for most labeled patients.
5. Reproductive-age women need hCG before elective surgery, whatever the contraception history.
6. Perioperative oral diabetes and ACE-inhibitor management: hold metformin and usually the ACE inhibitor on the day of surgery, and check glucose before incision.
7. In diabetic patients, keep an atypical cardiac presentation in mind for epigastric pain.
