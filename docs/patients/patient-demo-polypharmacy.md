### 2.2 Diagnosis and why

**Acute calculous cholecystitis, moderate (Tokyo Guidelines 2018 grade II).** It meets TG18 diagnostic criteria A (Murphy sign and RUQ tenderness), B (fever, raised WBC and CRP) and C (characteristic ultrasound), which together make a definite diagnosis.
- **Why grade II:** a palpable tender mass in the RUQ. WBC is below 18,000 and duration is under 72 h.
- **Why not grade III (organ dysfunction), so far:** creatinine is 1.9, *just under* the TG18 renal threshold of >2.0 mg/dL. However, **TG18 also counts oliguria as renal dysfunction**, and she reports reduced urine output. Measure urine output; she is one data point from grade III.
- **Cholangitis is unlikely:** normal bilirubin, ALP and transaminases, CBD 5 mm and no jaundice.

### 2.5 Chart-driven risks and how they change the plan

**Anticoagulation: apixaban 5 mg BID plus aspirin 81 mg (bleeding flag, high).**
- Last apixaban dose was at 07:00 today.
- **Her estimated CrCl (Cockcroft-Gault, 70 kg, Cr 1.9, female) is about 27 mL/min.** In the 2017 ACC periprocedural pathway, apixaban with CrCl 15–29 and an intermediate/high bleed-risk procedure has "no data": the pathway says to consider an agent-specific anti-Xa level and/or withholding **≥72 h**. With CrCl ≥30 the hold is ≥48 h. PAUSE used a 2-day hold for high-risk procedures but excluded apixaban patients with CrCl <25.
- **No heparin bridge.** Her CHA₂DS₂-VASc is about 6 (CHF, HTN, age ≥75 ×2, DM, female). Bridging adds bleeding without benefit.
- Her **apixaban dose is correct at 5 mg BID**: she meets only one of the three dose-reduction criteria (Cr ≥1.5; she is under 80 and over 60 kg).
- The **aspirin likely has no current indication** (no CAD, MI or stent). Stop it and discuss with cardiology.
- Sertraline adds a modest bleeding risk.
- Have reversal options known (andexanet alfa or 4F-PCC per institutional protocol) only if life-threatening bleeding or truly emergent surgery is needed. They are not used for convenience.

**TG18 operative timing vs fitness.**
- TG18 for grade II recommends early laparoscopic cholecystectomy in an advanced center *only if* **CCI ≤5 and ASA-PS ≤2**.
- She is ASA 3 or higher (AF, HF, CKD3, DM), with a Charlson index of about 6 or more (age 70–79 = 3, CHF 1, diabetes 1–2, renal disease 1–2 depending on version).
- **The TG18 flowchart therefore points to antibiotics and supportive care first, with early or urgent gallbladder drainage (percutaneous cholecystostomy) if she does not respond, and delayed cholecystectomy.**
- The apixaban washout window (48–72 h) is another reason to medically optimize first. Percutaneous drainage also carries bleeding risk on apixaban.
- A strong learner names this tension. Scalpal's authored plan is urgent laparoscopic cholecystectomy once anticoagulation is safely held and she is optimized, which is a defensible choice in a high-volume center. Jarvis should reward naming both paths.

**AKI on CKD3 (Cr 1.9 from 1.7; eGFR 27; K 5.1; HCO3 21), renal and metformin_renal flags.**
- Hold **metformin** (eGFR <30 is a contraindication, and there is lactic acidosis risk).
- Hold **lisinopril** and **potassium chloride** (K 5.1).
- Hold or reduce **furosemide** while she is dry, balanced against her HF.
- Avoid NSAIDs and IV contrast.
- Renally dose antibiotics and analgesics (avoid morphine accumulation; use acetaminophen, keeping track of the daily total).
- Use low insufflation pressures during laparoscopy to protect renal blood flow.
- Monitor urine output with a catheter; oliguria would make this TG18 grade III.

**Heart failure plus dehydration.** Give cautious, small-bolus fluid resuscitation with reassessment; she has bibasal crackles and edema. Continue **metoprolol** (rate control; avoid beta-blocker withdrawal). Get an ECG, troponin and chest X-ray. Insufflation and reverse Trendelenburg reduce venous return, so warn anesthesia.

**Allergies.**
- **Sulfonamide (high criticality, hives):** Avoid sulfonamide antibiotics (for example, TMP-SMX). She tolerates furosemide, a non-antibiotic sulfonamide, consistent with the evidence that cross-reactivity is not structural (Strom, NEJM 2003).
- **Contrast (low criticality, nausea):** Nausea is a common physiologic, non-allergic reaction. Avoid IV contrast anyway because of the AKI. An intraoperative cholangiogram gives little systemic exposure but needs an allergy plan.
- **Antibiotic choice for grade II:** For example, ceftriaxone plus metronidazole, renally adjusted per protocol. Avoid sulfonamides. No beta-lactam allergy is recorded.

**Diabetes (glucose 186 now; A1c 6.8%).** Hold metformin, use a sliding-scale or basal insulin plan, and check glucose before incision.

**Anemia (Hb 11.0) and type A negative.** Little reserve, and Rh-negative blood is less available, so the type and screen must be done early. Have crossmatched units if surgery proceeds.

**Polypharmacy (14 active drugs).**
- **Continue:** levothyroxine (it can be missed briefly), metoprolol, sertraline (avoid discontinuation syndrome; note the bleeding interaction), omeprazole (IV PPI if NPO).
- **Hold:** apixaban, aspirin, metformin, lisinopril, KCl, and furosemide (reassess).
- **Review:** trazodone (delirium and orthostasis risk), atorvastatin (continue when taking oral meds).
- **Elderly flag:** Delirium prevention, early mobilization, VTE prophylaxis timing after surgery once hemostasis is secure, and discharge planning now (she lives alone with a cane; her daughter is nearby).
- **Goals of care:** Karin is the health-care proxy, and Harriet's stated wishes should be documented before surgery.

### 2.8 Teaching points

1. **Cholecystitis vs colic:** constant pain over 6 h with fever and leukocytosis, after previous self-limited spells.
2. **TG18 grading drives management.** Grade II plus a high CCI or ASA means antibiotics first, drainage if she fails, and early surgery only in fit patients at advanced centers.
3. **DOAC interruption is renal-function dependent.** Calculate CrCl with Cockcroft-Gault, not eGFR. Don't bridge. Question unnecessary aspirin.
4. **AKI on CKD triggers a medication audit:** metformin, ACE inhibitor, K supplement, diuretic, NSAIDs, contrast, and renal dosing.
5. **Elderly diabetics present atypically.** Exclude MI. The fever may be blunted (hers is not).
6. **"Sulfa allergy" ≠ furosemide allergy.** Contrast nausea ≠ contrast allergy. Both still need to be documented and planned around.
7. **Polypharmacy reconciliation is a safety step.** Know what was taken *this morning*.
8. **Frailty and discharge planning start at admission.**
