### Case summary

Dolores Marchetti is a 63-year-old retired baker with type 2 diabetes (metformin), hypertension (amlodipine, metoprolol and an uncoded "blood pressure pill") and high cholesterol (simvastatin of unknown status). She has had three CT-confirmed episodes of left-sided (sigmoid) diverticulitis in 18 months. The last, in June, was complicated by a small pericolic abscess treated with antibiotics without drainage. Colonoscopy in August, after recovery, excluded cancer and showed left-sided diverticulosis. Her father had colon cancer in his seventies. She is quiescent now: afebrile, with mild deep LLQ tenderness only. She has no urinary symptoms suggesting a fistula and no red-flag bowel change. Medication reconciliation reveals that the uncoded "blood pressure pill" is hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily, and that she stopped simvastatin in the spring because of myalgias. Her chart creatinine was cancelled. Today's BMP shows potassium 3.4 and creatinine 0.9. The chart lists only "Amoxicillin allergy." She reports generalized hives (an immediate-type reaction) without anaphylaxis.

### Diagnosis and why

**Recurrent sigmoid diverticulitis, complicated by a resolved pericolic abscess.** Recommend elective laparoscopic sigmoid colectomy with primary anastomosis.

- The diagnosis is secure: three CT-confirmed episodes, always LLQ with fever, and a colonoscopy showing left-sided diverticulosis and no neoplasm.
- The indication is driven by complicated disease, not the count of episodes. ASCRS 2020 recommends that the decision to resect after uncomplicated episodes be individualized and not based on the number of episodes alone. After successful nonoperative management of diverticulitis complicated by abscess, elective colectomy is typically recommended because recurrence and complication risk is high. Her quality of life (three courses, one admission) supports the decision.
- Timing is appropriate. She is about 3 months out of the acute episode, the abscess has resolved on CT, and she is now quiescent with normal WBC and CRP. Elective resection is usually done at least 6 to 8 weeks after the acute episode.
- Extent: the whole sigmoid, with the distal margin on the upper rectum (where the taenia splay) to reduce recurrence.

### Chart-driven risks and how they change the plan

- **Uncoded medication** (chart: `"blood pressure pill, 1 daily"`, free text, no RxNorm). It is hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg. Thiazide plus mechanical bowel prep raises the risk of dehydration, hypokalemia and AKI. Check and replete potassium (3.4) before prep and surgery. Hold the thiazide on the morning of surgery (commonly also during prep). Encourage oral or IV hydration with the prep.
- **Statin of unknown status** (chart: simvastatin 20 mg, `status: "unknown"`, no date). She stopped it in spring because of myalgias. Reconcile so the anesthesia record is accurate. Statin intolerance goes back to her PCP (alternative statin). It doesn't affect the operation.
- **Cancelled creatinine** (chart: creatinine `status: "cancelled"`, no value, 2026-05-12). Kidney function was unknown until today's BMP (Cr 0.9, eGFR >60). She takes metformin plus a diuretic and faces bowel prep and a contrast CT, so this had to be checked. Hold metformin on the day of surgery and restart once she is eating with stable creatinine.
- **Diabetes, no A1c anywhere.** The chart has glucose 6.1 mmol/L (about 110 mg/dL, non-default unit) but no A1c, and the encounter `TESTS` list has none. Hyperglycemia raises surgical site infection and anastomotic complication risk. Get a preoperative A1c and use perioperative glucose control.
- **Amoxicillin allergy** (chart: "Amoxicillin allergy," no reaction, no criticality). The history is generalized hives, an immediate-type (likely IgE) reaction without anaphylaxis. Avoid aminopenicillins (ampicillin-sulbactam, amoxicillin-clavulanate). Cefazolin plus metronidazole remains appropriate IV prophylaxis because cefazolin's R1 side chain differs from amoxicillin's. (Cefadroxil shares amoxicillin's R1 side chain, and cephalexin shares ampicillin's, so avoid those two.) Ertapenem is an alternative. Oral antibiotic bowel prep (for example neomycin plus metronidazole) is unaffected.
- **Bowel preparation.** ASCRS/SAGES 2023 ERAS: mechanical bowel prep plus oral antibiotics is preferred before elective colorectal resection.
- **Beta-blocker.** Continue metoprolol perioperatively. Don't stop it abruptly. (The chart lists tartrate 25 mg with no frequency.)
- **Blood pressure.** 142 to 144 systolic on three agents, plus a thiazide-related potassium issue. This is acceptable for elective surgery and not a reason to delay.
- **Family history and cancer exclusion.** Already done (colonoscopy in August). Specimen pathology will confirm.
- **Ureter.** Inflammation from complicated disease can distort the left ureter. The `identify_ureter` step is the critical step in this procedure. Ureteral stents are selectively used in difficult reoperative or inflamed fields.
- **Stoma counseling.** An elective laparoscopic sigmoid resection in a quiescent patient is usually done with primary anastomosis, and a stoma is uncommon. Still, mark and consent for a possible diverting loop ileostomy. Her biggest fear is "a bag," so address it directly.

### Teaching points

1. **Messy charts hide risk.** A free-text "blood pressure pill" was a thiazide causing hypokalemia. A cancelled creatinine is unknown, not normal. A statin with "unknown" status had been stopped.
2. **The modern indication for elective resection** is complicated disease (abscess, fistula, stricture) or quality-of-life burden after shared decision-making. It is not a fixed number of episodes (the old "two episodes" rule is obsolete).
3. **Exclude cancer before resection.** Colonoscopy about 6 to 8 weeks after complicated diverticulitis (AGA 2021, ACP 2022).
4. **Screen for fistula** with pneumaturia, fecaluria, recurrent UTI and vaginal passage of gas or stool.
5. **Drug allergy specifics matter.** Hives to amoxicillin rule out aminopenicillins, not cefazolin.
6. **ERAS bowel prep** is mechanical plus oral antibiotics. Watch electrolytes in patients on diuretics.
7. **Patient myths.** Nuts, seeds and popcorn don't need to be avoided (AGA 2021). Fiber and activity are reasonable. A stoma is a common fear and should be discussed openly.
