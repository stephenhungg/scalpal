## Reference Kit

Nominal trocar access diameter also does not describe the outer diameter of its housing. Handle dimensions, wall thicknesses, masses, joint limits, and overall dimensions in the generic models are estimates unless explicitly sourced.

## Asset and Runtime Are Separate Deliverables

A useful tool has a Blender source file, a Unity importable mesh, physically meaningful scale, separated moving parts, material assignments, simple collision geometry, a grip transform, and a tip/action transform. Moving jaws require true pivoted parts, not a texture of an open tool. A high-resolution product render alone supplies none of the pickup, trigger, collision, or cutting behavior.

Render polished steel, dark insulation, differentiated handles, visible pivots, ports, grooves, and edges. For Quest, retain a simpler runtime mesh and simple colliders. Thin blades and shafts can use appropriately padded interaction volumes without changing visible geometry. Keep whole-body pickup separate from distal action contact. Do not use a detailed concave visual mesh as the default moving Rigidbody collider. Final polygon/material budgets require profiling on the physical headset.

The first meaningful interaction slice is scalpel/scissors plus a generic virtual practice surface, followed by grasper plus a movable virtual object. A material color change is feedback, not mesh separation. If the first implementation separates preauthored segments or draws an incision ribbon, label it as that behavior. Arbitrary deformable-organ cutting requires additional topology, deformation, collision, and contact work; meshes do not establish tissue mechanics or clinical fidelity.

Every irreversible virtual action should include the tool ID, target ID, contact point, activation state, session/attempt identity, and a result acknowledgment. Use an authored allowlist of tool/target/action combinations. Jarvis should explain actual acknowledged actions and current state rather than infer successful cutting from a spoken command.

## CV: Two Distinct Inputs

**Virtual tools:** Unity already knows their identity, pose, action state, and bounds. The raw passthrough RGB image contains the physical world and cannot contain later-rendered virtual tools. Project known tool geometry through the matching camera/view matrices for overlays and use scene IDs for state. A screenshot of the composited headset output can show virtual tools, but that is a different capture stream.

**Real tools or physical props:** A detector needs the intended physical classes, labeled examples, lighting/viewpoint coverage, and evaluation on real camera footage. The earlier bottle experiment does not validate this surgical catalog. Bounding-box detection alone also does not recover blade contact, depth, tool orientation, jaw state, or registration to anatomy.

Procedural Blender models can generate synthetic training images with class IDs, known poses, segmentation masks, and boxes derived from visible rendered pixels. Vary handles, materials, backgrounds, scale, occlusion, cameras, and illumination. Save calibration and world/camera conventions. Mesh bounds projected to an image can overstate visible extent or include hidden geometry; segmentation-based visible boxes and explicit truncation/occlusion labels are preferable for a labeled dataset. Synthetic render success is not evidence of real-tool recognition. Measure held-out real footage before claiming that the passthrough CV can recognize the tools.

## Boundaries of This Research

The sources above establish instrument families and selected shape/size references. They are not a procedural lesson, asset license grant, simulator validation, or evidence of surgical competence. Our person is a spatial reference: all cutting, energy, clamping, and closure effects apply to virtual simulation targets. Original generic meshes should not carry manufacturer branding or claim to reproduce a certified product.
