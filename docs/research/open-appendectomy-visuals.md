# Open Appendectomy Visual Acceptance and Simulator References

Research checkpoint: October 4, 2026. Scope: the ten open-appendectomy milestones in `docs/surgery-procedure.md`, in both AR and VR. This is a source-backed rendering specification, not a report that the current app passed a visual audit or that its tissue parameters are clinically validated. The actual scene needs stage captures and headset inspection against these criteria.

## What Needs to Look Real First

The useful target is a small, progressively exposed operative field with recognizable attachments and visible consequences. A polished atlas floating through the abdomen does not meet it. Prioritize location, exposure, contact and division continuity before adding cosmetic blood or higher polygon counts.

The clinical baseline is an incision in the patient's right lower abdomen, successive wall-layer exposure, protected peritoneal entry, delivery of attached ileocecal anatomy, vascular control, appendix division, inspection and closure. A classic oblique McBurney incision and a transverse Rockey-Davis incision are different approaches; the current case chooses the former. The incision must not become a line simply pointing from the hip to the navel. The university lecture places McBurney's incision across the ASIS–umbilicus connecting line. [Sohag University surgical lecture, incision and exposure sections, PDF pp. 12–16](https://staffsites.sohag-univ.edu.eg/uploads/419/1540723585%20-%20OPERATIVE%20STEPS%20IN%20OPEN%20APPENDICECTOMY%20FOR%20POSTGRADUATES.pdf#page=12)

## Stage-by-Stage Acceptance

The appearance column below is an **engineering interpretation** of the cited operative relationships. Suggested colors, shader effects and tests are our art-direction proposals; they are not measured tissue properties or universal clinical colors.

| Stage | Required appearance in the learner's view | Concrete failure to reject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Mark intact abdomen | Intact skin surrounds a short oblique RLQ mark. Hip and navel cues identify patient-right anatomy even when the learner stands on the opposite side. The mark follows the fitted torso surface. Optional teaching labels disappear without changing anatomy. | A central/umbilical window; a mark floating above skin; organs already visible; using screen-right instead of patient-right. |
| 1. Skin and fat | The cut starts where the blade crosses skin and extends along its actual stroke. There are two opposing skin edges, thickness at the edge, and irregular yellow fat beneath. Nearby intact skin stays opaque. Exposure grows locally; it does not immediately become a large oval aperture. | A rectangular shader hole outside the incision; duplicate wall/lip surfaces; identical opening length for a short puncture and full stroke; fascia/muscle already exposed. |
| 2. External oblique aponeurosis | The next exposed sheet is pale, fibrous and visually different from lobulated fat. A directional cue runs along the scored fiber direction, and the opening follows the tool action. | A smooth white rubber slab with no direction; a shader fiber direction that disagrees with scoring; an all-layer incision after a fascia cut. |
| 3. Muscle split | Retractors contact opposite edges and hold a gap. Reddish muscle has longitudinal fiber cues, with local stretch near the grips and coherent edges elsewhere. The split preserves muscle continuity outside the opening. | A scalpel-style missing chunk on successful blunt splitting; both tools pulling one node; retractors floating while a fixed ellipse opens; a single uniform fiber direction presented as all abdominal muscles. |
| 4. Peritoneal tent and entry | A thin, smooth membrane rises locally into a tent under forceps. The nick occurs at the elevated region, then enlarges into a membrane opening. Deeper tissue becomes visible through that opening. | The entire membrane rising rigidly; bowel passing through an intact membrane; membrane and bowel moving as one sheet; cavity visibility before entry. |
| 5. Deliver appendix | The exposed appendix is a curved, blind-ended tube attached at the cecum. A thin mesoappendix sheet and vessel accompany it. Pulling produces local traction, with the cecum remaining related to the ileum/colon. The delivered group fits through the opening without passing through the wall. | A free-floating worm; a giant cecal ellipsoid that hides the base; an artery detached from the meso; every organ translating as one rigid assembly without explanation. |
| 6. Control and divide meso | Clamp jaws visibly straddle the contacted tissue. The selected division creates separate edges at the measured site. Ties encircle the secured ends and remain after a clamp is removed. A failed unsecured cut shows a localized source and evolving fluid. | A scissors gesture with no changed geometry; a tie existing only in HUD text; blood emitted at wound center instead of the injury; a “secured” glow without an actual ligature cue. |
| 7. Base division and specimen | The cecal attachment, retained ligature, division site and distal/specimen clamp have a readable spatial order. Division leaves a short secured stump on the cecum and a distinct detached specimen with a cut end. The specimen can be moved independently after detachment. | The original whole appendix still present after removal; cecum disappearing with the specimen; a base-choice sphere unrelated to the attachment; a tied stump still connected through an invisible tube. |
| 8. Inspect and clean | Stump and mesenteric cut edge remain inspectable. Suction acts at its tip and reduces the local pool; ongoing flow refills it if the injury remains uncontrolled. Optional sponge appearance follows contact. | Suction erasing cumulative blood loss; a floor-sized flat red disk through bowel; persistent active bleeding hidden by a “clean” milestone; large decorative jets after a successful controlled cut. |
| 9. Closure | Deeper surfaces appose before superficial ones. The abdominal cutaway is removed after skin closure, leaving a seam, sutures or dressing. Assistance is visibly identified as auto-close. | Perfect untouched skin instantly returning with no closure cue; an open shader window over a closed wound; exposed fractured volume persisting after the patient is shown closed. |

Webop's surgeon-authored operative module provides accessible early-stage images and describes separate subcutaneous exposure, aligned external aponeurosis opening, blunt muscle separation and peritoneal entry. Its later operative videos require access; they were not read as if freely available. [Webop, open appendectomy technique](https://www.webop.de/en/general-and-visceral-surgery/colorectal-surgery/appendectomy-open)

The appendix arises at the posteromedial cecum and has a variable position. The taeniae convergence is a base landmark, so the visual base decision needs recognizable cecal geometry rather than arbitrary hovering targets. Mesoappendix is a folded attachment, not another cylindrical organ. [Webop, surgical anatomy](https://www.webop.de/en/general-and-visceral-surgery/colorectal-surgery/appendectomy-open/anatomy), [clinician-authored StatPearls appendectomy chapter, anatomy section](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK580514/?report=classic)

The university teaching sequence shows vascular control, a retained base ligature, a distal clamp, removal, field inspection and layer closure. These relationships support separate visual states for attached tissue, controlled tissue, divided ends and specimen. [Sohag University surgical lecture, PDF pp. 16–23](https://staffsites.sohag-univ.edu.eg/uploads/419/1540723585%20-%20OPERATIVE%20STEPS%20IN%20OPEN%20APPENDICECTOMY%20FOR%20POSTGRADUATES.pdf#page=16)

Technique disagreements should stay explicit: the university lecture discourages routine stump inversion, while Webop's module lists inversion and StatPearls describes it. Closure details also vary. Do not add an inversion animation, universal irrigation rule or new scoring threshold purely to match a reference image. Preserve the approved simple tied-stump case until a surgical reviewer selects a variant. The current <5 mm stump rule is supported by the chapter's complication discussion. [StatPearls, open technique and stump appendicitis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK580514/?report=classic)

## Material and Shape Direction for Quest

These are proposed visual cues, to be tuned on the actual headset rather than treated as clinical measurements:

- **Skin:** broad subtle roughness variation, restrained fine normal detail, a separate darker incision interior and damp cut edge. Do not add random scars, necrosis or patient-specific pathology to generic intact skin.
- **Fat:** clustered rounded lobules at multiple scales, uneven silhouette, warmer yellow/cream base with subtle pink vascular septa. More useful than uniform flat yellow bands.
- **Aponeurosis:** ivory-gray sheet, small aligned striations and low-frequency irregularity. Its texture basis must use the same material-space fiber direction as the mechanical rule.
- **Muscle:** darker red/pink fiber bundles. Use directional normal/roughness variation, not a flat red slab. Internal oblique and transversus should become distinct visual sublayers before calling the wall anatomically comprehensive.
- **Peritoneum/serosa:** smooth damp highlights and slight low-frequency color variation. A thin two-sided membrane with local tension reads better than global transparency that reveals the entire abdomen. Reserve translucency for a teaching overlay.
- **Cecum and ileum:** coherent hollow bowel shapes with a rounded cecal dome, visible attachment relationships and subtle surface vessels. The cecum needs taenia cues that converge on the appendix attachment. Avoid toy-like saturated red or blue organs in the default operative view.
- **Appendix and meso:** a tapered curved tube with a blind tip, a thin irregular attached sheet, and visible vascular paths. Case inflammation should be authored (redness/swelling, then separate gangrene or perforation variants), not randomized noise interpreted as disease.
- **Blood:** source-local ooze or arterial emission according to actual body state, shallow irregular pools constrained to visible surfaces, glossy boundaries. Pool appearance should affect visibility without filling the view with particles.
- **Metal/tools:** distinguish jaws, blade edge and handle; moderate metallic highlights, no mirror-like glare that hides contacts. Retractor tips must visibly support wound edges.

Wet-surface rendering research in a temporal-bone simulator identifies smooth specular reflections and fluid-boundary cues as useful realism features, and explicitly prioritizes interaction performance over extra rendering detail. That is evidence for testing damp highlights and local pools, not evidence that its bone shader or historical frame rate is suitable for abdominal tissue or Quest. [Kerwin, Shen and Stredney, *Enhancing Realism of Wet Surfaces in Temporal Bone Surgical Simulation*, author-hosted paper](https://www.osc.edu/sites/osc.edu/files/staff_files/kerwin/pdfs/kerwinTVCG_Sim_Realism.pdf)

## What the Current Asset Generator Can and Cannot Establish

`scripts/anatomy/build_targets.py` labels its output schematic teaching geometry. Its cecum is an ellipsoid with radii 26/25/33 mm; its terminal ileum is a constant-radius authored curve; some other ducts are explicitly enlarged for interaction. Materials are removed before export. The target preview assigns distinct object colors for identification, not clinical appearance. The representative small-bowel aggregate is explicitly incomplete. Therefore a successful export or watertight collider test does not prove operative realism.

Keep stable anatomy IDs and provenance. A replacement presentation mesh may add cecal shape, taeniae, serosal vessels and mesenteric contours while leaving the tested interaction proxy intact. But the attachment location, collision surface and longitudinal scoring stations must still correspond. Do not prettify the renderer into a different organ than the one the tool is touching. `export_briefing_atlas.py` already identifies a free-taenia source: verify whether the live OR consumes it before generating a duplicate or claiming it is absent.

## Implementation Order and Evidence

1. **Exposure and closure correctness:** one wound contour in fitted coordinates, no rectangular perimeter gaps, no duplicate surface fighting, only the next layer exposed, no post-close cutaway. Use the measured incision instead of a fixed success ellipse.
2. **Action visibility:** local peritoneal tent, latched retractor contact, visible ligatures, measured division seams, retained stump and detached specimen. Visuals consume the accepted body facts; they do not create scoring facts.
3. **Anatomy continuity:** cecum/ileum/appendix/meso connections and mobile deformation. Add local attachment constraints before enlarging the atlas or applying global rigid translation as realism.
4. **Materials and lighting:** shared tissue shader with per-layer parameters, fiber basis, restrained wetness and small reusable detail textures. Prefer opaque stereo-safe shading over several overlapping transparent layers.
5. **Consequence presentation:** injury-local bleeding/contamination, suction at the tip, pool containment. Keep finite limits and test the bad path as well as the perfect path.

For every stage, capture the actual `NativeSession` once from a wound close-up and once from a learner-height oblique view. Include held/latched tools and existing HUD. Record commit, stage facts, camera pose and whether actions were real runtime tool fixtures or staged body events. Test short/off-center incisions, reversed approach side, registration rotation and reset. Compare division before/after with identical cameras. Require image differences plus semantic assertions so a missing or blank renderer cannot pass.

Then inspect the same beats in the physical Quest for stereo depth, shimmer, readability, occlusion, tool response and frame time. Editor screenshots cannot establish those properties, AR fit, tactile fidelity or clinical training transfer. A qualified surgeon's review remains the appropriate way to assess operative content once the visuals and actions are coherent.

## Reference Access and Reuse

The clinical teaching links above are references, not imported asset licenses. Do not download/repackage their operative photos or textbook illustrations as app textures or Git assets. Original procedural meshes/textures and properly licensed anatomy can reproduce the anatomical relationships. Webop's freely accessible early-stage images are useful for exposure comparison; its remaining video content is access-controlled. The functioning Sohag PDF filename contains `1540723585`; the similarly indexed `1540722938` URL redirected to an account-suspended page during this research. NCBI's `sites/books/.../?report=classic` endpoint was accessible when the shorter endpoint presented a browser challenge.
