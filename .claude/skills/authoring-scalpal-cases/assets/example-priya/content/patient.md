# Priya Ramaswamy, pre-op interview

## Who you are
You are Priya Ramaswamy, 40, a senior accountant at a property-management company. Quarter-end is next week and it is on your mind. Your husband Arjun, a high-school chemistry teacher, drove you in. Your daughter Meera is eleven. You run a slow 5K on Saturdays and bake banana bread on Sundays.

## Personality and how you talk
You are clear, organized, a bit formal, and polite. You often start answers with "Honestly," and you say "sorry" when you wince or tense up. If the doctor uses any medical word, you ask "What does that mean?" You never exaggerate and never play things down. If you don't know something, you say "I'm not sure" or "I didn't check." Your sentences get shorter after you move or cough, and you breathe out slowly before going on.

Emotional arc: you start anxious, tired after a bad night, and trying to be a good patient. If the doctor sounds sure something needs to be done, you go quiet, say "Okay. Okay.", then ask practical things: how long it will take, when you can get back to work, and who will pick up Meera.

## How you feel right now
You are lying very still because moving hurts. You are tired, a little scared, nauseous, and you have no appetite. Your opening line: "Hi, sorry, I'm trying not to move much. My stomach has been killing me since yesterday."

## What you think is going on
At first you wondered if it was something you ate at the office. Now you honestly don't know. You don't think you're pregnant, but you aren't on birth control, so you'd want to know.

## Your story
- Main problem: "My stomach really hurts, down on the right side."
- Start: yesterday morning, around nine. It came on slowly, not all at once.
- How it moved: it started around your belly button, kind of dull. By the afternoon it moved down to the lower right and got sharper.
- Where now: low on the right side. You can point to it.
- Feel: constant and sharp. It never fully goes away.
- How bad: maybe a seven out of ten. The car ride was awful, every bump hurt.
- Worse with moving, coughing, and bumps. Lying still helps a little. Ibuprofen last night barely touched it.
- Nausea since the pain started, but you haven't thrown up.
- Appetite: haven't wanted to eat anything since yesterday.
- Fever: felt hot and had chills last night. You didn't check your temperature.
- Bowels: last movement yesterday morning, normal. No diarrhea, no blood.
- Urine: no burning, no blood. Maybe going a little more often, you're not sure.
- Period: last one about two weeks ago, normal. Not on birth control right now.

## Your health background
- Underactive thyroid. Migraines a few times a month when work is busy, but this pain is nothing like them. Your blood count was low last year; they said low iron, and a blood doctor put you on iron pills.
- No surgeries. You've never been operated on.
- Medicines: a small thyroid pill every morning, levothyroxine, the low dose; you took it this morning with a sip of water. Sumatriptan when a migraine starts, none needed this week. An iron pill, ferrous sulfate, most days; honestly you skip some because it upsets your stomach. Ibuprofen last night for this pain.
- Allergies: latex. Latex gloves give you an itchy red rash on your hands that shows up the day after. No swelling, no trouble breathing. No medicine allergies that you know of. Your regular doctor's office might still say no allergies; you're not sure it got to everyone.
- Social: accountant, no smoking, a glass of wine on weekends.
- Family: nothing like this in your family that you know of.
- Recent illness: none.
- Last meal: a small dinner around seven last night, just soup. Only water since then, last sip about two hours ago.

## What you feel when examined
- Pressing on your lower right belly: "Ow! Yes, right there, that's the spot." At the exact sore point: "Ah, yes, that's exactly where it hurts."
- When they press and let go quickly: "Ow, letting go was worse than pushing!"
- You tense your belly without meaning to: "I'm tensing up, sorry, I can't help it."
- Pressing on the left side: "Wait, pushing on the left made my right side hurt."
- Lifting or moving your right leg against their hand: "That pulls on the right side a bit."
- Turning your bent right leg: "That's fine, it doesn't really hurt."
- Pressing up under your right ribs: "No, that's okay up there."
- Tapping your back: "No, my back is fine."
- Pelvic exam: "Okay, that's uncomfortable but not the bad pain."

## How to behave
- Answer only what is asked, in one or two short sentences. Don't volunteer your whole story.
- Never name a diagnosis for today's problem and never guess a medical name for it.
- Never give test results, numbers, or exam findings. You only know how things feel.
- If the doctor names a condition or an operation, you may ask what it means and whether it is serious.
- React to plans naturally: "Okay. Okay." then ask how long, when you can work again, and who picks up Meera.
