## 2. CLINICIAN KEY (never given to the patient agent; for Scalpal and authors)

### 2.1 Case summary

Priya Ramaswamy is a 40-year-old woman with hypothyroidism, migraine and iron deficiency anemia. About 26–28 hours before she arrived, she developed periumbilical pain that migrated to the right lower quadrant, with anorexia, nausea without vomiting, and subjective fever and chills. On exam she is 37.9 C, HR 98, with focal RLQ tenderness at McBurney's point, rebound, voluntary guarding, and positive Rovsing and mild psoas signs. The pelvic exam is benign. WBC is 13.1 with 82% neutrophils and CRP is 48. Serum beta-hCG is negative and the urinalysis is clean. Ultrasound did not show the appendix, but the ovaries are normal with normal flow. CT shows an 11 mm appendix with periappendiceal fat stranding and no abscess or free air. Her records come from two health systems (Northstar primary care; Quillhaven endocrinology/hematology), and they disagree: one lists no known allergies and the other lists a latex allergy.

### 2.2 Diagnosis and why

**Acute (uncomplicated) appendicitis.** The evidence:
- The classic visceral-to-parietal migration of pain, with anorexia and nausea following the pain.
- Low-grade fever, leukocytosis with a left shift, and a raised CRP.
- Localized peritoneal signs.
- CT findings that are diagnostic, with no features of complication (no abscess, phlegmon or free air).

Her Alvarado score is about 9 of 10: migration 1, anorexia 1, nausea 1, RLQ tenderness 2, rebound 1, temperature ≥37.3 C 1, WBC >10 2, left shift 1. That counts 2 for RLQ tenderness and is high on any version of the score. She is a woman of reproductive age with an equivocal ultrasound, so CT was appropriate after pregnancy was excluded (WSES 2020).

### 2.3 Must-not-miss differential (discriminating features in this patient)

| Diagnosis | Why consider | What argues against here |
|---|---|---|
| Ectopic pregnancy | Reproductive age, RLQ pain, no contraception | Serum beta-hCG negative (must be ordered; history alone is not enough) |
| Ovarian torsion | Sudden, severe unilateral pelvic pain, nausea | Gradual onset with migration; ultrasound shows normal ovaries with flow; no adnexal tenderness |
| Ruptured/hemorrhagic ovarian cyst | Mid-cycle (LMP ~2 weeks), RLQ pain | Migration pattern, fever, leukocytosis; no free fluid; normal ovaries |

### 2.5 Chart-driven risks and how they change the plan

- **Latex allergy (Quillhaven: criticality low, contact dermatitis; Northstar: "No known allergy").** Scalpal's brief raises this to a high-severity latex flag, so the room, gloves, catheters and every device must be latex free, and she should be the first case of the day if possible. Contact dermatitis (type IV) is far less dangerous than type I anaphylaxis. Even so, standard practice is a latex-safe environment for any documented latex allergy, and the conflicting records are themselves the lesson: **reconcile allergies with the patient, don't trust a single source.**
- **Iron deficiency anemia (Hb 11.6, ferritin 9 in Nov 2025, on ferrous sulfate).** She has a modest reserve, and an appendectomy carries low blood-loss risk. A type and screen is sufficient. Restart iron after discharge.
- **Hypothyroidism on levothyroxine 50 mcg (TSH 3.1–3.4, euthyroid).** No change to the plan. Levothyroxine has a long half-life, so a missed dose or two while she is NPO is fine.
- **Migraine on sumatriptan.** No perioperative issue. Note that triptans plus serotonergic antiemetics carry a theoretical serotonin risk; this is mostly a reconciliation point.
- **Recent NSAID (ibuprofen last night).** Not a meaningful bleeding contraindication for laparoscopy.
- **Fasting:** Clear water until about 2 hours ago and a light meal at 19:00 the night before, so she meets standard fasting intervals. Confirm the timing with anesthesia.
- **Antibiotics:** Give standard prophylaxis or treatment for uncomplicated appendicitis (for example, cefazolin plus metronidazole, or per local protocol). There is no drug allergy.
- **Duplicated medication across sources:** Levothyroxine appears twice (Northstar 2026-03-02, Quillhaven 2026-03-05). The brief dedupes it by code; the learner should still name it once on reconciliation.

### 2.7 Socratic probes for common misses

- **No pregnancy test:** "She told you she doesn't think she's pregnant. What would it cost you to be wrong?"
- **Allergies not asked:** "Her primary care chart says no known allergies. Did you ask *her*? Two systems are feeding this chart."
- **Went straight to CT:** "What do you need to know before you put a 40-year-old woman in a CT scanner?" Then: "What did the ultrasound actually help you rule out?"
- **Torsion dismissed without reason:** "What on her ultrasound makes torsion unlikely?"
- **Anemia ignored:** "Her hemoglobin is 11.6. Is that new? Does it change what you'd order before the OR?"
- **Crohn's never considered:** "She has iron deficiency. Is there anything in her history that would make you think about the terminal ileum instead?"
- **Urgency mislabeled as emergency or elective:** "She's stable, without perforation or abscess. How fast does she need to go, and what do you start while she waits?"
- **No last-meal history:** "Anesthesia calls you. When did she last eat and drink?"

### 2.8 Teaching points

1. In a woman of reproductive age with lower abdominal pain, **get a beta-hCG every time**, before imaging and surgery.
2. Migration of pain is among the most specific historical features of appendicitis. Anorexia and nausea typically *follow* the pain.
3. In adults, ultrasound is a reasonable first test for women (to look at the ovaries) but often does not show the appendix. CT confirms the diagnosis when the ultrasound is equivocal.
4. **Multi-source records conflict.** The Northstar chart lists "No known allergy" and Quillhaven lists latex. Absence of an allergy in one system is not evidence of no allergy. Reconcile with the patient.
5. Latex contact dermatitis (type IV) is different from type I latex anaphylaxis, but either way the OR is set up latex-safe.
6. Uncomplicated appendicitis can be managed non-operatively with antibiotics in selected patients (WSES 2020). Laparoscopic appendectomy remains the standard, and Scalpal's plan is appendectomy.
